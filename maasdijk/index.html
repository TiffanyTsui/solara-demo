Maasdijk

Three greenhouses, one physical model each, built from the measured buildings and the settings the grower runs. The model was calibrated at Made and moved here without refitting. It reconstructs the indoor climate from 5 January 2026 on observed weather, extended every morning, and continues each timeline three days ahead on the day's weather forecast. The measured weeks score it afterwards; they never feed it.

The three greenhouses

How to read this page

  • One model per greenhouse. Inside a greenhouse the compartments differ from each other by about 0,6 °C in daily mean. The model cannot see that difference, so an error of that size is the floor for any model that sits outside the compartment.
  • The solid line is the model on observed weather, the dots are the sensor. The model runs every hour from 5 January to the last day KNMI has published, about one day behind. A dot appears only on days an export exists for that compartment.
  • The dotted line is the forecast. Every morning at 09:00 one issue per greenhouse runs the same model on the Open-Meteo weather forecast at the site (the KNMI Harmonie blend, not a KNMI product) from the last observed day through three days ahead. The seam is marked. An issue is frozen with its weather and never edited; the next morning's issue replaces it on the chart.
  • Three errors, one per line. On a scored day the forecast is judged against the sensor (the total), and the same day's model on observed weather is judged against the sensor (the model share). The difference between the two lines is the weather share, the part of the miss the forecast weather caused. The three add up. Scoring uses the last issue made before the day, so a day-ahead forecast, and only full measured days, so rows appear when an export arrives.
  • The weather share also holds a station difference. The observed line runs on KNMI Rotterdam, 17 km away, because Hoek van Holland publishes no cloud cover; the forecast runs at the site itself. Part of the weather share is that distance, not the forecast.
  • Zoom in and the lines turn hourly. The buttons above each chart and the slider below it set the window; under 14 days the daily means give way to the hourly model, forecast and sensor, which is where the afternoon peak shows.
  • The daily maximum runs 1–3 °C cold. The model reaches the afternoon peak late. The maximum is compared hour to hour, the hottest measured hour against the hottest modelled hour, so the gap is the model's and not the sensor's quarter-hour sharpness. The same shortfall exists at Made and is where the next calibration round goes. Daily means carry the score; the maxima are in the day tables under each chart, so the shortfall stays visible.
  • Heating and venting are one setting all year. The grower raises both for some weeks after a planting. Those weeks read cold in the model, in whichever compartment was just planted.
  • Kas 2 and Kas 3 are the two halves of one building, split along the central path. Which half holds afdeling 5–7 is not recorded; the model does not depend on it.
  • A forecast number in the header needs seven scored days. Until then the header says the forecast is running. One or two days say more about the weather than about the model.
  • Where the numbers live. Every figure here is written from the model repository by one script, once a day. Corrections go there first; this page follows.
